J.A.R.V.I.S. is a desktop application that works on your own computer. Most of it never sends anything anywhere. This page lists everything that can leave your machine, when it leaves, where it goes, how long it stays and how to remove it.

Effective 18 October 2026. J.A.R.V.I.S. is made by Alan Araujo. Questions and requests about your data go to alanvitoraraujo2a@gmail.com.

What never leaves your computer

Your code and your Git repositories
J.A.R.V.I.S. opens folders that are already on your disk and runs the git you already have. It does not upload, clone or mirror them.
Terminals, conversations and session logs
Everything an agent writes, every conversation and every session log stays in the app's data folder on your disk. See Where your data lives.
Provider logins
The logins of your Claude Code and Codex accounts stay in those tools' own configuration folders. J.A.R.V.I.S. never opens, copies or sends them.
Your voice
Dictation runs on your computer. Audio is never sent anywhere.

The AI agents you run — Claude Code, Codex — talk to Anthropic and OpenAI directly, with your own accounts and under those companies' own privacy policies. J.A.R.V.I.S. does not sit in the middle of that traffic and does not receive it.

The optional account

You never need an account to use J.A.R.V.I.S. If you create one, it lets your notebook, projects and preferences follow you to another computer. The account lives on a server we run on Railway.

What we receive when you create it

With Google
We ask Google only for openid, email and profile, and keep your Google account ID, your verified email address, your name and the address of your profile picture. The picture is not shown anywhere. Google's access tokens are used once, to confirm who you are, and are not kept.
With an email and a password
Your name, your email address and your password. The password is stored only as a salted scrypt hash; nobody can read it back.
Your session
Signing in creates a random token on your computer. The server keeps only a hash of it. It expires after 90 days, and signing out revokes it.

What is copied to the server while you are signed in

While a computer is signed in, it keeps these up to date on the server automatically. Signing out stops it.

  • Preferences: theme, language, terminal font size and scrollback, the autopilot turn budget, notification switches, the performance overlay, and whether new agents open as a chat or a terminal.
  • The notebook: every notebook and every note, with its full text.
  • Projects: each project's name, its folder path on your disk, its autonomy level and whether it is archived — but not its files.
  • Project notes, project knowledge and guardrail rules you wrote.
  • Workspace settings: account rotation, the global autonomy level, starting minimised, the local model's settings (the Ollama address and model name) and which projects and sessions were open, by ID.
  • The provider accounts you added: the provider, the label you gave it, the email address of that provider account, its order and whether it is paused.
  • A usage snapshot for each provider account: its label, email, plan and sign-in state; its usage windows and when they recover; tokens per day; how many sessions are running; prices; and the provider's message when it refuses work.

Never copied: provider logins, your code, terminal output, agent conversations and session logs.

The community

Only if you create a community profile. Everything you put in it is public: anyone on the web can read it, without signing in.

Your profile
The handle, name, bio, location, website, GitHub user, stack and what you are building — the fields you fill in. Your email address is never shown.
What you publish
Posts, prompts and replies are public. Likes, saves, follows, blocks and reports are kept to make the community work. Someone who saves your prompt keeps a copy in their own notebook.
Statistics and presence
Private unless you choose followers or everyone. Statistics are totals and model names — never project names or folder paths. Presence is how many sessions are running and when you were last active.
Deleting
A deleted post stops being shown, but its text stays in the database until you delete your profile. Deleting your profile removes it together with your posts, likes, saves, follows and blocks.

The phone companion

Only if you pair a phone. The computer then leaves a short summary in a mailbox on Vercel, where the phone reads it and leaves your answers.

  • What is sent: the computer's name; for each project with work under way, blocked or failed, the project name, the mission title, its status, why it is blocked and how many sessions are running; and for each approval waiting for you, the kind of operation and the first 120 characters of the command it wants to run.
  • What is not sent: terminal output, file contents and conversations.
  • How it is stored: as plain data in private storage — not end-to-end encrypted. Pairing tokens are kept only as hashes.
  • How long: the phone treats a summary as stale after an hour and an answer after five minutes, but the latest summary stays stored until you unpair. Unpairing, in Settings → Phone, deletes the mailbox.

Downloads the app makes when you ask

Check for updates downloads a small file from GitHub, and the voice model is downloaded from Hugging Face when you install it. Those services see your IP address, as with any download.

This website

No cookies and no analytics. It remembers your theme and language in your browser's own storage, and never sends them. Vercel hosts it and, like any host, handles your requests to serve the pages.

Who else handles the data

Only the services that run the parts above: Railway (the account and community server and its database), Vercel (this website and the phone companion), Google (signing in with Google) and GitHub (update downloads). We do not sell data, show advertising or use analytics, and we share nothing with anybody else unless the law requires it.

How long it is kept

Account data stays until you ask for it to be deleted. Sessions expire after 90 days. A note or document you delete stays on the server marked as deleted, so your other computers learn it was deleted; a deleted document keeps no content, and a deleted note may. Our server records only errors; Railway and Vercel keep their own request logs under their own policies.

Your choices and your rights

  • Use it without an account. Nothing above happens until you sign in, create a community profile or pair a phone.
  • Sign out to stop all copying from that computer.
  • Delete this account, in the app, removes the account from that computer and signs it out. What the server holds stays until you ask us to delete it.
  • Ask us, at the address at the top, to see, correct, export or delete what the server holds about you. We confirm by reply when it is done. These are your rights under Brazil's LGPD, and we honour them wherever you live.

J.A.R.V.I.S. is a tool for software developers and is not meant for children.

Changes
