Why Autonomy sits above Guardrails

How much an agent does on its own is the broader question. What it may never do is the fence around that answer. Reading the fence first makes no sense.

Why Guardrails is its own section

It is one row per operation the core reports — a list free to grow without this screen being touched. A section that can get longer on its own does not share a page with anything else, or it will eventually push everything below it out of sight.

A project has settings of its own

The project workspace has a Settings area hosting the project-scoped autonomy and guardrail controls. Those panels are literally the same components as the global ones, given a project id — which is why the two can never drift apart in behaviour or in wording.

The panel that had a parameter and never received it

The guardrail panel had accepted a project id since guardrails shipped and had never once been given one, because global Settings renders only when no project is open and structurally cannot host a project-scoped control. The capability existed; the place to use it did not.

The audit, and what it found

Three values the product used, showed on screen, and gave nobody a way to change:

  • The two upper levels of the autonomy chain. Resolved project → global → default, with neither of the upper two reachable.
  • The autopilot turn budget. The panel rendered turn 3 of 24 while 24 was a constant in the source.
  • Terminal type size and scrollback. Hard-coded.

All are now settings, and none of them were new features — they were things the product already did, made adjustable.

Bounds are enforced in the core

Two directions, two behaviours

Out of range on the way in is refused, so a stored value is always one somebody could have chosen. The surface renders a slider, so an out-of-range value should be impossible — which is exactly why it is not trusted: a command reachable from the webview is reachable from any bug in it.

Out of range on the way out is clamped, so an older build's row cannot produce an unusable terminal or a zero budget that fails every run before it starts.

Unset is no row, and a value that will not parse reads as unset rather than failing the screen.

No accordions inside a section

The left column already answers click and more options open. A second level of hiding buries more, not less.

Verified in the real build

All eight sections, dark and light, both languages, at 1440 / 1086 / 746 / 606 px. Four defects existed only on screen and not in the stylesheet: the section floating 215px from its map, the selected row invisible in the light theme, the active chip off-screen in the narrow strip, and every panel asking the viewport how wide it was when the pane is 344px narrower than the window.

Control alignment was checked by sampling pixels rather than by eye: all five settings controls share one left edge at x=581.

The settings reference lists every setting with its default, its bounds, and what happens the moment you change it.
